How to cancel a free trial before you get charged

By Chiagozie Oliver, founder of EZER · Updated Nov 21, 2026

Cancel where you signed up: Google Play, Apple, or the company's website. Do it early: Apple says to cancel "at least 24 hours before the trial ends". Deleting the app does not cancel it. If you already got charged, cancel now so it doesn't renew again, then ask about the charge. EZER warns you before a free trial turns into a bill, so you can cancel in time (Get EZER on Google Play).

Where to cancel

You signed up throughWhere to cancel
Google Play (Android)Google Play subscriptions, pick the trial, tap Cancel subscription
Apple (iPhone, iPad)Settings, tap your name, Subscriptions, tap the trial, Cancel Subscription
The company's websiteSign in on the website and cancel from the account or billing page. Store menus won't show it.
A TV, console or another provider (Roku, Amazon, cable)Cancel with that provider. The charge is on their bill, not the app maker's.

Sources (checked Nov 21 2026): https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481, https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428

When to cancel

Right after you sign up is safest. Apple's page says to cancel "at least 24 hours before the trial ends". On Google Play, Google says "When you cancel a subscription, you'll still be able to use your subscription for the time you've already paid." Check each service's terms, because some end trial access the moment you cancel.

Deleting the app doesn't cancel the trial

Google says uninstalling an app does not cancel its subscription. The trial still converts and bills your card.

Already charged?

Cancel now so it doesn't renew again. Then ask the company about a refund; for Google Play and Apple purchases, ask through the store. If you never agreed to the charge, call your card issuer. Steps: how to stop recurring charges on a credit card.
