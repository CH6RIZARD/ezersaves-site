How to stop recurring charges on a credit card

By Chiagozie Oliver, founder of EZER · Updated Nov 21, 2026

Cancel with the merchant first and keep the confirmation. If the charge came through Google Play or Apple, cancel it there, because the merchant can't. If it keeps billing after you cancelled, call your card issuer, then dispute it in writing so the letter reaches them within 60 days of the first bill that shows it (FTC). 1. Find every recurring charge first

Go through the last three months of each card and look for the same merchant on the same day of the month: streaming, apps, gyms, software, and free trials that turned into a bill. Annual plans only show up once a year, so check twelve months if you can. EZER does this part for you on Android in the US: it connects through Plaid (read-only) and lists every recurring charge on your cards in one place.

2. Cancel with the merchant

Sign in to the service and cancel from its account or billing page. Screenshot the confirmation or save the email. The CFPB says stopping payments does not cancel the contract itself, so cancel the service, not only the payment (CFPB). In EZER, each subscription shows its cancel page and the steps to get there.

3. Billed by Google Play or Apple? Cancel there

If the charge on your card says Google or Apple, the merchant can't stop it. On Android open Google Play subscriptions, pick it and tap Cancel subscription. On iPhone: Settings, your name, Subscriptions. Google says uninstalling an app does not cancel its subscription.

Sources (checked Nov 21 2026): https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481, https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428

4. Still charging? Call your card issuer

Tell the issuer you cancelled and give the date and the confirmation. Ask what they can do about the charge and future ones. A new card number can stop charges tied to the old one, but don't count on it alone: cancel the service too.

5. Dispute it in writing

For a billing error, the FTC says to write to the card company and "Include your name, address, account number, and a description of the mistake." Send it "so that it reaches the issuer within 60 days after the first bill with the error was sent to you", and "Use the address given for billing inquiries, not the address for sending your payments" (FTC). Attach your cancellation confirmation.

Debit card or bank account instead?

Different rules. The CFPB says to tell the company you are revoking authorization, tell your bank, and give the bank a stop payment order "at least three business days before the payment is scheduled". Banks commonly charge a fee for it.

Sources (checked Nov 21 2026): https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/using-credit-cards-and-disputing-charges, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/you-have-protections-when-it-comes-to-automatic-debit-payments-from-your-account/, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-i-see-a-charge-i-dont-recognize-en-1605/
